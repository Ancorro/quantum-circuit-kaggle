## Why the quantum circuit competition

- Task: shrink three fault-tolerant quantum subroutines (inverse square root for
  fusion, Reed-Solomon decoder for DQI (Decoded Quantum Interferometry),
  FeMoCo block encoding for chemistry), written as kickmix circuits.
- Score: geometric mean over the three tasks of reference cost / your cost.
- Verifier: exact, classical fuzz test, CPU only, open source (`kickmix` on PyPI).
  Hosted scoring takes ~45 min; local checks take seconds to minutes.
- The hosts built it for agents: companion paper arXiv:2610.23832
  (Khattar, Rubin, Gidney et al.). A related paper, arXiv:2610.31352
  (Crichigno, Flammia), ran Claude agents in a "verified autoresearch loop".
- Rules: 5 submissions/day, team up to 5, external tools allowed, winners
  release code under Apache 2.0.
- Prizes: 1st-6th $50k/25k/10k/5k/5k/5k; $10k per task for best single task;
  $10k milestone (top public score on 2026-11-20, notebook must be public);
  $10k longest leader (public notebook).

## Read first

- mrmeows notebook and dataset (`qcc-1375-open-circuits`, Apache 2.0).
- Forum: "What didn't work: 30-odd measured nulls" (alex chilton).
- Forum: "There is room for local models too: a one-person setup with a limited AI budget".
- Forum: "dfthcsos: why lambda_eff can't go much below ~46.8".
- dqi verifier: use `--max-workers 2` (about 6 GB per worker); Windows runs
  verifiers one at a time, so use WSL or the HPC `share` partition.
